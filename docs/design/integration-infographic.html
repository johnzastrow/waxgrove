Waxgrove · streaming integration

Two friends.
Two services.
One playlist.

Nobody needs both streaming services. Nobody installs anything. Across a complete round trip you touch a music app twice — everything else happens in Waxgrove.

01 — The shape of it

The group spans the gardens, not the person

Waxgrove never asks you to hold two subscriptions. It asks the group to span them — and then stands in the middle, storing songs by platform-neutral identity so neither service is privileged.

Why this works at all

A streaming service makes a song's identity its own ID. Waxgrove stores the ISRC and MusicBrainz ID instead, and treats provider IDs as a disposable cache. So a playlist survives leaving a service — or Waxgrove itself, since playlists export as JSPF.

02 — Setup, once

Connecting your own service

Spotify and Apple Music work differently, because their rules differ. Neither path is required — Waxgrove is fully usable with no service connected at all.

Why the detour?

Spotify only grants wider access to organisations with 250,000+ monthly users. A friends' app can never qualify — so everyone brings their own key. Their rule, not ours. The upside: no user limit, and the request budget is yours alone.

Reading is free

Finding songs on Apple, showing artwork and opening tracks in Apple Music all work with no membership and no key. Only writing a playlist into Apple Music needs the $99/yr developer membership — and the instance owner pays it once, not each person.

You also need an Apple Music subscription

The membership is the operator's. An active Apple Music subscription is yours, and without it playlists cannot be written to your library.

Copy a link in Spotify once. Later, going the other way, tap Add to Library in Apple Music once so Waxgrove can see the playlist. That is the entire footprint inside the streaming apps — no exporting, no CSV files, no re-typing track names.

Going the other way is slightly easier

Apple Music lets Waxgrove list your playlists, so you pick one from a menu. Spotify no longer allows that, which is why the Spotify direction needs a pasted link. Same result, one extra tap.

05 — Honest limits

What will not work, and why

Some songs won't transfer

Licensing differs by service and by country, so a handful of tracks in any large playlist will be missing on the other side. Waxgrove tells you which ones and why rather than quietly handing you a shorter playlist — and the full playlist always stays intact here.

Syncing takes a minute

The free music database Waxgrove uses allows about one lookup per second, so a 45-song playlist takes a while the first time. It runs in the background — close the tab. And it gets faster as your group adds music, because songs already known are matched instantly.

No workarounds, deliberately

Waxgrove uses only official, documented APIs. No scraping, no shared accounts, no reverse-engineered endpoints — those break constantly and put the project on the wrong side of the services' terms. Where a service offers no usable API, playlist files are the bridge.
