Waxgrove
Sonic Unwalled Garden Library Exchange

A record collection that grows in the open. Grooves and growth rings are the same shape — one mark that reads as both, and never closes.

01 — The concept

A grove is a garden without a wall

“Walled garden” is the exact phrase for what this project rejects, so the brand is built on its opposite. Wax is the record, in collector register — crate digging, first pressings, the shelf. Grove is an open stand of trees: grown, not built, and no fence. Every ring in the mark carries a gap. Nothing here closes.

02 — Colour

Warm analog, not cold neon

Deep desaturated grove green on wax black, with label-paper cream and a copper accent doing the work that neon does elsewhere.

Wax 900#0B0C09 · ground

Cream 200#E5DCC5 · label
Copper#C07B45 · accent
Hard constraint — never Spotify green (#1DB954).

A vivid green music app reads as a Spotify clone, which is precisely the wrong signal for a project defined by not being a walled garden. The primary green here is deliberately dark, desaturated and closer to moss than to lime — copper carries the energy instead. Also excluded: Apple Music’s pink-red gradient, and neon-on-black streaming aesthetics generally.

03 — Typography

Letterpress warmth over cold geometry

Mid-century record-label print is the reference. A wonky optical serif for display, a humanist sans for interface, and mono for the catalogue layer — ISRCs, MBIDs, confidence scores.

04 — The ring system

The logo is the confidence indicator

This is the idea worth remembering. The mark isn’t decoration bolted onto the product — it is the product’s core mechanic. Waxgrove’s hardest problem is match confidence, so the rings encode it: complete grooves mean a certain match, broken rings mean the record needs your judgment. The brand appears on every row of the app, doing real work.

Exact

ISRC or MBID matched. Unbroken grooves. Automatic — no human needed.

Strong

MBID Mapper, high score. Small gaps. Accepted, but auditable later.

Uncertain

Fuzzy match below threshold. Rings fracture. Sent to disambiguation.

Unresolved

Raw text only. Kept in the crate — never silently dropped.

05 — Screens

Mobile-first, four core surfaces

Designed at phone width first. Cream “label” cards sit on wax black; the catalogue layer stays in mono so identifiers never masquerade as prose.

Mobile-first, not mobile-only.

The phone sets the design order, but desktop is a first-class target — the PWA installs there too, and several tasks are genuinely better with room: bulk crate curation, working through a long import queue, reordering a large playlist. These four screens are the phone tier only. Desktop layouts still need designing, and a stretched phone column is not a desktop design — the crate in particular wants a two-pane treatment (sources left, staged items right) that has no phone equivalent.

06 — Guard rails

What this must never look like

Spotify green

#1DB954 and anything near it. The single fastest way to make this read as a clone of the thing it exists to escape.

Streaming neon

No gradients, glassmorphism, or dark-mode purple glow. This is a printed object, not a dashboard.

Music clichés

No music notes, equaliser bars, soundwave squiggles, headphones. The rings already carry the meaning.

SaaS default

No thin geometric sans, no generic card grid, nothing that reads “AI startup”. Warmth and weight over sleekness.
